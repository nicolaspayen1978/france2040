# Publication and consultation

1 October 2026. Decision in force. The published research is the consultation. The website is the canonical repository of the Pacte, not a brochure that points at files.

## Two cycles

Research runs **Research → Publication → Discussion → Revision**.

The site runs **Pacte 2040 → Documents → Discussion → Revision**.

Publication comes before the work is necessarily settled. Narrative pages may explain the Pacte. They cite documents. They do not establish or withdraw a claim.

## What is published

Everything substantive is a document: the proposal, appendices, Red Teams, bank benchmarks, calculations, assumptions, source notes, scenario tests, and the record of revisions.

Every document is versioned. Every document is public by default. Every document is commentable, once the discussion layer exists. Every claim should be traceable to evidence.

A text that is not part of the reasoning — a contact list, a private note — stays off the site. The Decision Log, the DFMA note, the deployment strategy, the thesis note, and the social-contract sketch are not yet documents on the site. Once a text is a document of the Pacte, it is public, including while its conclusion is open.

## Document and discussion

A document is a stable, versioned text. Comments sit beside it. They never alter it. A revision is a new version, published on its own, saying what changed and why.

HTML is the text that is cited. Word and Excel are attached source artefacts.

The unit of reference is a version plus an anchor. An anchor is a paragraph, a table, a list item, or a heading in that version. A criticism of that passage stays pointed at it after later edits, because the later text is a different version.

A published version is immutable. The working file in `Docs/` may continue. Publishing copies it into a snapshot. The snapshot is not edited in place.

## Status is not a verdict

Document status is one of: **Working paper**, **Under review**, **Frozen**, **Superseded**.

Public labels: document de travail, en relecture, gelé, remplacé.

Status is the life of the text. The verdict inside the text is separate. A working paper may say that a conclusion is not established. Frozen passages inside an open test do not make the document frozen. Frozen, as a document status, means this work will not be revised. Superseded marks a version that has a successor. Every published version stays addressable.

## What a document page shows

- current version, publication date, and update date
- document status
- the verdict the text itself reaches, including an open one
- sources named by that version
- revision history
- the HTML text, with a stable anchor on each passage
- the address of that version, so a disagreement can be linked

## Discussion — specified, not built

Comments wait until a real document page is accepted as the way Pacte research should exist in public. The first page is Red Team 05. No comment is stored before that.

When the discussion layer is built:

Anyone may submit. A name is required. Organisation or affiliation is optional. Email is verified before submission. The comment then waits in a short moderation queue.

Moderation covers relevance, abuse, spam, and legality. It does not ask whether the comment supports the Pacte. A substantive objection stays visible.

A comment is on the whole document or on one anchor. Its kind is **Question**, **Objection**, **Evidence**, or **Suggestion**. It records the document, the version, and the anchor.

An author’s response is not the status of the issue.

- Response: **No response** or **Response published**.
- Issue, independently: **Open**, **Addressed in vX**, or **Contested**.

Publishing a reply does not mean the objection has been disposed of.

## First build

HTML → metadata → stable anchors → sources → versions → revision history.

No comments. The public text is the note as it stood on 1 October 2026. Its verdict remains the one written in the note.
