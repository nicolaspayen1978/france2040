# France 2040 — Banking Economics and Financial Stability

## Can a €700bn interest-only home-equity market be an attractive banking asset without becoming a new systemic risk?

23 October 2026. Working file. Not a public snapshot. Not an épreuve contradictoire. Not a cell in the macro model. Not a claim that lenders would want the asset, and not a claim that the design is safe.

Structure frozen the same day. Do not lengthen this framework. The next work fills evidence: capital treatment, funding economics, substitution, and loss behaviour. It does not add a further bridge.

Feasibility study. The economics question is whether the supply side has a risk-adjusted reason to originate the product. The stability question is what new risks a large interest-only market would place on lenders and on whoever ends up holding the asset, set against the risk of the amortising credit it replaces. Either answer may be no. A conclusion that the current design fails is a result. The product is not changed in this note to avoid that result.

The €700bn scenario represents gross Pacte credit outstanding by 2040. It should not be interpreted as €700bn of additional bank lending or revenue. Part of the new credit would refinance or replace existing mortgage balances. This note estimates the genuinely incremental credit opportunity and tests whether its risk-adjusted economics could be attractive to lenders.

Those estimates are not yet made. The cells below stay empty until each one has a source, a tagged assumption, or an explicit scenario split. The emptiness is the list of evidence this paper does not yet have. It is not a draft to be filled with rough margins.

## How the opening sentence sits on the frozen path

The stock used here is the frozen end-2040 outstanding of the interest-only share, about €700bn. Under the 20-year reference in Red Team 05, no contractual bullet of that book falls before 2040, so cumulative origination of the share and the stock outstanding are the same number. That identity is an artefact of maturity. It is not an identity with incremental bank assets, and it is not a revenue figure.

Two different substitutions stay separate. Without that separation, the €700bn can be attacked as double counting.

## Four questions

### 1. How much of €700bn is genuinely incremental?

This question is the system-credit bridge. It measures household secured credit. It does not measure what banks earn.

€700bn gross Pacte outstanding → *€Xbn* substituted existing debt → *€Ybn* genuinely additional household credit.

**Outright owners.** No housing loan remains on the dwelling. The advance is largely new secured credit: the banking system gains an asset, and household secured debt rises by the advance. “Largely” is not “entirely”. Some of these households hold other debt, and some of the advance may consolidate an unsecured balance. That residual is a measurement, not an assumption of zero.

**Existing borrowers.** Two slices, kept apart.

- Refinancing of the outstanding balance. The new contract replaces principal the household already owes. System-wide household credit does not rise by that principal. If the same bank replaces its own loan, its assets do not rise by it either. If another bank refinances it, the asset migrates. Migration is a competitive result, not incremental credit for the system.
- Additional equity extraction. Principal above the replaced balance. That slice is incremental household credit.

Headcount is not the euro split. Outright ownership of the main home is 37.4% of households, and 81.9% among couples aged 65 or over, against 20.0% of households still repaying (`09_RedTeam_01_Equity_Reservoir.md`). Those shares do not allocate the €700bn. Housing credit to individuals was €1,289bn at end-July 2026. That stock is the encumbrance being partly replaced or left in place. It is not €X.

Whether an existing borrower can add a tranche without refinancing is the open coexistence sub-test in Red Team 05. About two-thirds of the housing-loan book is cautioned rather than mortgaged, so a second real right is not a mechanical product. Until that sub-test is decided, €X is a scenario split, not an observation. The household side of the same choice — a legacy rate around 1–2% can make refinancing privately costly — is already in the parcours note. The bank’s gain and the household’s gain need not have the same sign.

Purchase finance stays outside this stock when an amortising loan accompanies the purchase. The frozen wording counts only the interest-only share. Cannibalisation of amortising production that would otherwise have been written is a displaced asset in the bank-economics bridge, not a deduction inside €X.

System incremental banking assets equal genuinely additional lending only if the substituted balances remain inside the banking system and no asset leaves it. Bank-level assets also depend on who wins the refinance. Those two increments are reported separately when the bridge is filled. They are not filled by allocating 37.4% of €700bn to outright owners.

### 2. What does the resulting asset generate for banks?

Incremental system credit and incremental bank economics are different bridges. Measuring income on the additional assets only would miss the substituted book.

A contract can add nothing to system credit and still change the lender’s result. Illustration, not a measured book: €200,000 of an existing loan at 1.5% — a rate inside the 1–2% legacy range already filed, not an average of the stock — replaced by €200,000 of Pacte principal at the frozen 3.30% rate on new housing loans, with no equity extraction. Incremental system credit on that contract is zero. Yield, duration, funding, capital profile and runoff on that €200,000 all change. The household may lose the old rate. The bank’s gain and the household’s gain need not have the same sign. 3.30% is not established as the price of the Pacte.

**Bank-economics bridge**

Economics of the whole relevant Pacte book — yield, duration, funding need, capital profile and runoff, on the substituted slice and on the additional slice.

Minus the economics of the assets displaced — remaining margin, life, capital and runoff of the replaced loan, and of the annuity that would otherwise have been originated.

Minus incremental funding, hedging, operating, loss and capital costs.

Equals incremental economic contribution.

Each term is empty. The lines inside the subtraction:

1. Funding cost, at a funds-transfer price. The internal prices named in the four 2025 prudential readings are unpublished. Do not invent one, and do not use €23.1bn as the starting income.
2. Credit losses. Low loan-to-value makes the collateral case easier to state. It does not supply a French expected-loss rate. The Dutch observation that losses at four large banks rose by about 0.2% of book in a different interest-only market is not an input. The French loss channel stays unwired. The loss is compared with the loss on the amortising book displaced, not only stated on its own.
3. Operating cost of origination and of servicing a loan that does not amortise, net of the operating cost of the asset displaced.
4. Capital required. No risk weight is imported. Standardised and internal-model treatments are both unread for this product. A junior lien, if that is the security, may not take the same weight or the same funding price as a standard residential mortgage. That difference is already an open item in the legal note. Capital is the requirement of the Pacte book minus the requirement of the displaced book, not a weight applied only to €Ybn.

Contribution is that residual. It is not a spread times €700bn, and it is not a spread times €Ybn.

### 3. What does the bank give up?

The give-ups are the subtraction in the bank-economics bridge. They are not a second, hidden adjustment.

- **Displaced amortising mortgages.** Two cases. Replacing a live balance gives up the remaining margin and life of that loan. The economics of the new contract on that same principal still enter the first term of the bridge: a euro-for-euro refinance can raise or cut contribution. Originating the interest-only share instead of an annuity the household would otherwise have taken gives up the annuity’s margin, runoff and capital profile. The sign is not assumed.
- **Balance-sheet capacity.** Additional credit uses a place on the consolidated balance sheet. A euro-for-euro refinance need not expand assets and can still consume more stable funding, capital and hedge capacity, because duration and risk weight can change. Red Team 05 has not answered how much expansion the system can carry. This note does not answer it either. It records capacity as a constraint on the incentive, not as a spare number taken from a liquidity ratio.
- **Duration and asset-liability exposure.** Absent prepayment, interest-only principal remains until maturity. The frozen comparison at 3.30% is a 20-year contractual life against 11.13 years for the annuity. Observed French covered pools are shorter still, because they amortise, season and prepay. Contractual life is not economic duration. On a long fixed-rate principal that does not amortise, the borrower holds an option: refinance or repay when that fixed rate is expensive relative to the market, and remain when it is cheap. That is adverse duration convexity for the lender. No behavioural prepayment rate is imported. The cost of hedging or of carrying the mismatch belongs in the bridge if it is not already inside the funding cost.
- **Capital and liquidity consumption.** Equity and stable funding used by this book are not available for another asset. The comparison is with the consumption of the displaced asset, and with an alternative use of the same equity.

### 4. Would banks actually want to originate it?

Compare the Pacte asset with ordinary new French housing credit on five terms together: return, capital, expected loss, duration, liquidity. Then compare the return on the capital consumed with the next use of that capital inside the bank, if a published or stated hurdle exists.

The ordinary new loan is originated at about 78–80% loan-to-value. A Pacte advance might be underwritten at a lower consolidated ratio. A consolidated 35–40% is an example to test. It is not a result, and it is not the 40% cap on the interest-only share. That cap does not limit the consolidated debt-to-value ratio. Red Team 08 is frozen on the distinction.

Interest-only lengthens duration and leaves a bullet. A lower consolidated ratio, if it is actually underwritten, can improve the collateral-loss case relative to an amortising loan at origination ratios near 80%. The research is allowed to find a worse maturity profile and a better collateral-loss profile. It has not found it. Whether the trade-off is attractive is the empirical question.

The figure a lender uses is incremental contribution divided by incremental CET1 consumed, set against the same ratio on ordinary mortgage lending. €Zbn of contribution is not that ratio. Favourable residential treatment, or worse treatment of the actual security, can move it enough to change the conclusion. Neither treatment is assumed. The test clears only if that return, after funding, losses, operating costs, hedging and liquidity, is preferred to both alternatives on the five terms. A gross margin above a funding rate does not clear it. A positive contribution at system level does not mean each of BNP Paribas, Société Générale, Crédit Agricole and BPCE would originate. Who can write a same-bank additional advance, and who would have to refinance another lender’s cautioned loan, can decide the distribution before the system average does.

No return is estimated in this version.

## Risks

Risk is a purpose of the note, not a closing box. The note looks for risks the design would add, and for risks it would replace. An asset can show a high contribution and still be a bad asset once default, collateral, maturity, funding, capital, conduct and the location of the risk are counted. None of the rows below is mitigated by assumption.

The 40–50% cap on the interest-only share is not a guarantee that the claim stays inside the collateral value. Red Team 08 is frozen on that point: a fall in the property erodes the cushion around a principal that does not amortise; loss and default depend on debt service, the consolidated debt-to-value ratio, rank, cautions and procedure. This note does not rerun that test and does not fill a loss rate.

### Correlation

Low loan-to-value can make one loan look safe. €700bn secured on the same national housing market is a common exposure. A national recession can produce, in the same window, falling house prices, income stress, weaker capacity to refinance, fewer transactions, and pressure on bank funding. That joint movement is not the question “is an individual loan at 40% loan-to-value safe?” It is the question Banque de France and the HCSF would ask of the book. It is not estimated. It is not answered by adding the separate red-team results together.

### Maturity wave

If each vintage matures a fixed term after origination, contractual principal later reproduces the origination curve. It does not arrive as one repayment of €700bn. On the frozen path, January booking at 20 years shifts the table above by twenty years: €25bn due in 2047, €45bn in 2048, €65bn in 2049, €80bn in 2050, €90bn in 2051, €90bn in 2052, then the same decline, to €0 in 2060. December booking starts in 2048. A 10-year contract brings the wave inside 2027–2040. A 30-year contract starts it in 2057 or 2058. Red Team 05 froze that arithmetic and stopped.

The chart to draw, once and only once the channels exist, is origination 2027–2040 against maturity 2047–2060, with an overlay of sale, cash repayment, refinancing, estate, and other. Every channel is empty. House-price appreciation over twenty years is not assumed, and it would not by itself pay the principal. Low loan-to-value at origination makes the collateral case easier to state. It does not assign the wave to a channel.

Whether the wave is dangerous is not decided here. The quantity is. A phased rise in new credit does not spread the bullets if every vintage has the same contractual life: the rise and the later decline are copied, not flattened.

No combined stress is run. Red Team 08 does not simulate a path of prices, defaults or bank losses. The combined-failure test announced as EC-09 has not been conducted. This note does not start it, and it does not convert a −30% price move into a loss.

### If the design fails

The permitted conclusions include: the return is too weak to originate; the return is adequate and the maturity wave, the correlation, the funding or the loss profile is still unacceptable; the collateral-loss case is better and the maturity case is worse, and the trade-off fails; the risk sits somewhere other than the originating bank and is not thereby solved. Any of those is a binding constraint. Any of those stops the claim that the €700bn stock would be supplied.

A passing result would make the scenario more credible. It would not, by itself, make the stock happen. Household willingness and an originable product would still have to hold.

Staggered maturities, amortisation later in the life, a sinking fund, an age rule or an insurance wrap are not studied and are not adopted. If a test fails, the place where the contract would have to change can be named. Making that change is a later decision. It is not a way to make this test pass.

## Could the stock change the structure of French mortgage banking?

The lender’s return is the first question. It is not the only one. A long-duration, low loan-to-value secured pool of this size would sit inside a market that is, on the figures already filed, amortising, fixed-rate and caution-heavy: €1,289bn of housing credit to individuals, 99.4% of July 2026 production at fixed rate, average loan-to-value at origination around 78–80%, and cautions on roughly two-thirds of the book. Covered-bond funding is one component of that system, not a loan-by-loan match, and current pools run off faster than a non-amortising contract.

The structural questions, each open, and none of them a finding:

1. **Funding.** Does a persistent principal change the mix of deposits, covered bonds, senior debt and retained issuance that now finances amortising pools? Red Team 05 still has to answer financeability. This note asks whether the structure of that funding would be different, once financeability is known.
2. **Covered bonds and securitisation.** Current programmes are built on amortising collateral with behavioural prepayment. A low loan-to-value interest-only pool is a different asset for investors. Eligibility of a general-purpose advance for a société de financement de l’habitat remains open in the legal note. Eligibility is not demand.
3. **Institutional investors.** Insurers and other long-liability investors might want a long fixed secured asset. That is a hypothesis. No investor mandate has been read against this collateral.
4. **Competition.** A same-bank additional advance and a refinancing of another bank’s loan do not give the four groups the same opportunity. The 2006–2014 hypothèque rechargeable is a warning that a legal facility can fail to become a market. The reasons it failed are not fully separated, and banking incentives are one of the unread reasons (`24_Home_Equity_Credit_Standard.md`).
5. **Monetary-policy transmission.** French housing credit is already overwhelmingly fixed-rate, so this product would not introduce fixation. What it would add is a principal that does not run off and a contractual payment equal to interest. For participating households the cash-flow channel of a later rate move is then limited to loans that refix or mature. For banks, the mismatch sits in the asset-liability book. Whether that changes aggregate transmission is unestimated. It is not asserted.

These five questions are why the note is addressed to the lending groups, the Banque de France, the HCSF and economists. A sentence of the form “the proposal generates a stated annual sum for French banks” answers none of them, and it collapses gross margin into profit.

## Research chain

€700bn scenario → who borrows it → how much replaces existing debt → how much is genuinely new credit → what each euro of Pacte assets earns versus what it displaces → how much capital and funding it consumes → who ultimately holds the risk → what happens under correlated stress → what happens when the bullets mature → whether rational lenders would supply the required volume.

A yes makes the scenario more credible and does not execute it. A no is another binding constraint, and it can show where a later redesign would have to start.

## Classification

1. **Established.** The end-2040 interest-only stock in the 20-year reference is about €700bn, and the annual path from €25bn in 2027 to €0 in 2040 sums to that stock, with a peak of €90bn in 2031 and 2032. At 3.30% the contractual interest on the stock is €23.1bn a year. That interest is not a margin. January booking at 20 years reproduces the path as contractual principal from 2047 to 2060. Gross peak origination is about 61% of 2025 housing-loan production excluding renegotiations. That percentage is a flow neighbourhood, not incremental credit. Outright owners and borrowers still repaying are different populations, on the shares already filed. Mass origination is not available under the current HCSF decision. The four groups’ published liquidity positions are not spare capacity.
2. **Plausible, not shown.** A material part of any real book would be new credit from outright owners, and another part would replace outstanding balances. Substitution can change bank economics while adding no system credit. A low loan-to-value, long fixed pool could matter for funding instruments, institutional buyers, competition and the cash-flow channel. None of those shares or effects is measured.
3. **Weak hypothesis.** Any particular return. Any claim that the economics are attractive. Any claim that the maturity wave is dangerous, or that it is manageable. Any claim that the collateral-loss profile is better than ordinary housing credit. Any claim that the stock would restructure French mortgage banking.
4. **Potentially fatal, not shown.** Lender economics too poor, once displaced assets are subtracted, for banks to originate at the scenario scale. Capital treatment of the actual security that collapses the return despite the interest income. Prepayment that shortens the asset when the fixed rate is expensive and lengthens it when the rate is cheap, enough to spoil the hedge. A maturity wave whose sale, repayment, refinancing and estate channels cannot carry the recopied path. Correlated stress that makes individually conservative loans systemic together. A loss, funding or conduct profile that is unacceptable even if the return clears. A book banks want, which still fails household take-up or the HCSF test. Risk moved to investors without being reduced. Any one of these stops the stock. None is demonstrated.
5. **Data required.** A household split of existing secured balances between outright owners and borrowers, then, for each vintage, the share refinanced versus added as a tranche. The same microdata foundation as EC-10, cut for substitution rather than for originatable collateral. The economics of the displaced assets, not only of the new ones. A funds-transfer price, an operating cost, a French expected-loss rate at the relevant loan-to-value, and a capital and liquidity treatment for the security actually used, each compared with ordinary housing credit. No risk weight is chosen in advance. The four result cells: €Ybn, €Zbn, €Cbn, and contribution per euro of CET1 against ordinary mortgage lending. For prepayment: behaviour when the fixed rate is expensive and when it is cheap, and the effect on realised life, hedge effectiveness and funding. Not an imported constant prepayment rate. For the wave: the shares repaid by sale, cash, refinancing, estate and other, in the years the frozen bullets fall due, and who holds the claim if the originator does not. For correlation: one joint case of prices, incomes, refinancing capacity, transaction liquidity and bank funding. Not five separate narratives added up.

EC-10 remains the originatable-stock test. It is not opened here. Red Teams 05 and 08 are not reopened. EC-09 is not started. This note does not enter the Phase 2 workbook.
